# Interaction research · 8 October 2026

The design goal is a sparse meal app that feels responsive, clear and pleasant. The studies below inform design choices; they do not establish that this app is more satisfying. Two independent design agents read original papers and reviewed the app's current flows. Their recommendations prioritize assignment, the result of an edit and the completed split.

## Findings and limits

| Original research | Relevant finding | Application and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Tuch et al., 2012: visual complexity and prototypicality](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en/us/pubs/archive/38315.pdf) | Two studies with 59 and 82 participants rated briefly presented company-website screenshots. Low complexity and recognizable structure received the highest beauty ratings. | Retain native forms, navigation and disclosures. Frame existing meal icons quietly and strengthen the split's existing total. Passive website viewing and a young sample do not prove an iOS interaction preference. |
| [Ng et al., 2012: low-latency direct touch](https://www.tactuallabs.com/papers/designingLowLatencyDirectTouchInputUIST12.pdf) | Ten participants could distinguish very small response delays while dragging on custom hardware. The paper explores immediate feedback separated from subsequent processing. | Assignment and calculated previews update directly; animation does not postpone the final value or block another input. Its hardware and dragging task supply no latency target for this app. |
| [Heer and Robertson, 2007: animated transitions](https://idl.cs.washington.edu/files/2007-AnimatedTransitions-InfoVis.pdf) | Twenty-four chart-familiar adults performed object-tracking and value-estimation tasks. Animation helped tracking and several estimation conditions; elaborate staging sometimes made errors worse. | Use one brief native transition when an existing amount changes. Omit initial counting, staged entrances and repeated effects. Chart experiments do not establish financial comprehension or a suitable iOS duration. |
| [Hassenzahl, Diefenbach and Göritz, 2010: needs and positive experiences](https://hassenzahl.wordpress.com/2010/07/23/needs-affect-and-interactive-products-%E2%80%93-facets-of-user-experience/) · [original paper text](https://assets.website-files.com/6130bc5b67f7afc3311fd697/61ecc672e7e91dceae8bb1cd_Needs%2C%20affect%2C%20and%20interactive%20products%20%281%29.pdf) | A retrospective German questionnaire collected 548 completed positive technology experiences. Need fulfilment correlated with positive affect. | Let people see and control the result of allocation. Completion copy describes assignment rather than praising the user or implying payment. Positive-only retrospective reports do not demonstrate causality or test these interface features. |

Apple’s [selected-row styling contract](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/environmentvalues/backgroundprominence) resolves semantic foreground styles against prominent selection backgrounds. Direct iPad inspection exposed fixed dark metadata on the selected meal. A semantic secondary-style comparison then produced near-failed ordinary phone contrast reports. Library people/date now use native primary styling; their smaller type preserves hierarchy. An independent designer reviewed this correction; fresh contrast and selected-state validation are recorded separately in verification.

Apple's [fluid-interface guidance](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/803/) emphasizes response, continuity and interruption. Its [haptic guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics) emphasizes an intelligible cause, consistent meanings and restraint. The [system feedback API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/sensoryfeedback) supplies selection feedback; the system decides whether compatible hardware and settings permit playback. These are platform design references, distinct from controlled studies.

## Implemented design hypotheses

| Flow | Change | Observable contract |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assign an item | **Everyone** selects the current people in one draft action and finishes price entry. Individual switches remain editable; the shortcut is absent with fewer than two people and disabled when everyone is selected. | No write before Save. Cancel preserves saved assignments. The selected people and complete price remain visible after the keyboard dismisses. |
| Understand a draft | Selected people show their exact item shares immediately. Invalid price drafts remove the preview. | Preview and saved calculation use the same stable-ID cent allocator. Shares exclude tax and tip; no hidden model is created or mutated to preview. |
| Follow an amount edit | Native numeric transitions connect an existing value to its replacement. | Values and accessible semantics update immediately. Motion is finite and limited to value changes; Reduce Motion removes it. The 220 ms choice is a design judgment, not a research-derived threshold. |
| Finish allocation | A check and **All items assigned** appear on meal and split when the valid, nonempty bill is completely assigned. | Empty, invalid and unassigned zero-price bills do not claim completion. Assignment says nothing about payment or successful share delivery. |
| Share the result | Split total uses the meal's established hierarchy; **Share split** is a visible native primary action. | The complete share label and control remain reachable at large text sizes and in landscape. Invalid-value sharing remains disabled. |
| Recognize a meal | A subtle backing frames the existing meal icon. | No duplicate graphic or legend; decorative content stays hidden from accessibility and is omitted when larger text needs the width. |
| Feel a bulk choice | Native selection feedback accompanies the explicit Everyone action. | No additional effect on each native toggle, on typing, Cancel, failed Save or opening a screen. System settings and hardware determine playback; simulator execution cannot certify tactile quality. |

## Review criteria

Native interaction checks cover Everyone, an individual exception, Cancel, saved values and process relaunch. Calculation checks cover odd cents, stable remainder order, invalid boundaries and completeness. Fresh captures must show normal and largest-text phone/iPad layouts, the item preview's accessible name and on/off state, readable sharing, narrow-sidebar icon layout and both appearances. A native Reduce Motion check must preserve the dedicated simulator's original setting.

Source review and older captures do not validate this slice. [Verification](verification.md) records current-source outcomes, fresh screenshots and recordings alongside remaining gaps. Visual preference, correct allocation, runtime responsiveness and physical haptic perception remain different judgments; no numerical satisfaction improvement is claimed.

Final native review found a dark iPad selected-row contrast defect: native white labels inherited the global mint selection fill. A Home List scoped deep teal tint preserves readable selected labels while keeping the existing mint detail action. This choice uses Apple's [subtree tint API](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/view/tint(_:)); [final captures](evidence/satisfaction-final-media-provenance.json) verify the actual rendered state on iOS 27. It is a bounded native-state correction, not a guarantee about every List style or a measured satisfaction result.
